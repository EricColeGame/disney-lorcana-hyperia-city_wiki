## The Six-Point Scorecard for Judging Legendary Cards

Instead of chasing a tier list that goes stale in three weeks, score each legendary against six repeatable criteria. This is the same framework many competitive players describe using when a new set drops, and it works whether you're evaluating the Disney Lorcana Hyperia City best legendary cards or a reprint from an older set.

| Criterion | Question to ask | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Ink identity fit | Does it reward being in its own two-ink pairing, or fight it? | High |
| Lore efficiency | How much lore per point of ink, and does it quest the turn it lands? | High |
| Board impact | Does it change the board the turn it arrives, or only later? | High |
| Card advantage | Does it replace itself or generate recurring value? | Medium |
| Versatility | Does it work in more than one archetype? | Medium |
| Recursion potential | Can you replay, shift, or bounce it for repeat value? | Medium |

A card that scores high on the first three is usually a genuine staple. A card that only scores on versatility is usually a "sideboard" legendary — useful, but not worth chase pricing. Player experience suggests most overhyped legendaries fail the board impact test: they look incredible in a vacuum and arrive one turn too late.

## Three Tests to Run Before You Spend a Dime

You don't need fifty games to know whether a legendary is real. Three quick tests will get you most of the way, and they cost nothing but a proxy and an evening.

### Test 1: The Two-Ink Check

Lorcana decks run a maximum of two ink colors. Ask honestly whether the card is good in *both* of its likely pairings or only one. A legendary that's only correct in a single color combination is a niche card wearing a staple's price tag.

### Test 2: The Curve Test

Write the card into a realistic curve. If it costs enough that you're skipping a quest turn to play it, it needs to win the game shortly after. Cards that "set up" without immediate lore or board impact tend to underperform in faster local metas.

### Test 3: The Removal Test

Ask what happens when your opponent deals with it immediately. If the answer is "I lose my entire plan," the card is fragile. If the answer is "I still have a board and a card in hand," you've found something durable.

| Green flags | Red flags |
|---|---|
| Questing value the turn it lands | Needs a full turn of setup first |
| Two-for-one or better on resolution | Dies to common removal with no value |
| Fits multiple ink pairings | Only works in one narrow shell |
| Recurs or shifts for repeat value | Expensive with no immediate impact |
| Covered in community tournament reports | Hyped only in reveal-season discussion |

## Budget, Timing, and Trade Strategy

Pricing on chase legendaries follows a predictable arc: reveal-season hype, a spike around early tournament results, then a slow settle as supply catches up. Community reports consistently show the steepest premiums in the first couple of weeks, which is exactly when most players buy. If your goal is playing rather than collecting, patience is the highest-value skill in this hobby.

| Approach | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Buy singles at release | Competitive players who need a deck now | Paying peak hype pricing |
| Wait four to six weeks | Budget players and brewers | Prices rising if a card tops an event |
| Trade duplicates for staples | Anyone with a deep binder | Trading away a card that later spikes |
| Buy a box and trade the extras | Players who enjoy the opening experience | Box variance rarely beats buying singles |
| Proxy first, buy later | Testing whether a legendary fits your deck | Forgetting to actually buy it eventually |

A practical habit: keep a short watchlist of the Disney Lorcana Hyperia City best legendary cards you actually intend to play, not every card that looks exciting. Five names is plenty. Check prices weekly, buy when a card dips after its first hype wave, and skip anything you can't describe in one sentence of deck strategy.

## Building Around a Legendary: A Quick Checklist

Once you've identified a legendary worth building around, the rest is discipline rather than discovery. Work through this list before you buy a single support card:

1. Confirm the ink pairing and check that the deck's mana base supports it.
2. Count how many copies you need — three or four for a true anchor, one or two for a utility effect.
3. Identify two cards that protect it or recur it from the discard.
4. Test against the fastest aggro deck in your local scene; if it folds, adjust the curve.
5. Compare the legendary to the closest non-legendary alternative and justify the price gap.
6. Re-evaluate after ten games — the meta moves, and so should your list.

That last step is where most players stall. A legendary that was correct in week one can be a liability in week six, and the Disney Lorcana Hyperia City best legendary cards list will look different in a settled format than it does during reveal season. Treat every ranking, including this framework, as a starting point rather than a verdict.

## FAQ

**Are the Disney Lorcana Hyperia City best legendary cards worth buying at release?**

Usually only if you need them for an event in the next two weeks. Release-window prices carry a hype premium that almost always fades. If you're playing casually, waiting a month is the better financial play.

**How many legendaries should a competitive deck run?**

Most lists run somewhere between four and eight legendary copies across two or three different cards. More than that and you start crowding out the cheap, efficient commons and uncommons that make a deck function.

**Do Enchanted versions play differently?**

No. An Enchanted card is an alternate-art treatment of the same card and uses identical rules text. You're paying for collectibility, not power, so buy the standard printing if your goal is competing.

**What's the fastest way to test a legendary before buying?**

Print a proxy and play ten games with it in a real deck. Player experience beats any tier list — if the card underperforms in your local meta, it doesn't matter how highly the community rates it.
