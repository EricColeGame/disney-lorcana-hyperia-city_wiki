## Rarity Tiers and What They Actually Change

New and returning players conflate rarity with power. They are not the same thing. The Disney Lorcana Hyperia City card reveals are a good teaching example, because the closing wave of premium treatments generated far more excitement than the mechanically strongest cards in the set.

| Tier | What it is | Who it is for | Practical impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common and Uncommon | The bulk of the gallery, easy to pull | New players, budget builders | Fuels limited play and entry-level constructed decks |
| Rare | Mid-tier staples and role-players | Most constructed players | Where the real deck-building value usually sits |
| Super Rare and Legendary | Powerful, lower pull rate | Competitive players and traders | Drives single prices early in the set's life |
| Enchanted / Epic treatments | Alternate-art collector printings | Collectors and investors | Scarce, visually distinct, and largely irrelevant to gameplay |

The takeaway is simple: build your deck from the commons, uncommons, and rares, and treat the top tier as a hobby-within-a-hobby. If you are on a budget, waiting two to three weeks after launch is almost always cheaper than buying into week-one hype.

### A Note on Sealed vs. Singles

If you want specific cards, buy singles. If you want the experience of opening packs and the chance at premium treatments, buy sealed — but treat that money as entertainment spending, not investment. Community reports consistently show that chasing one card through packs costs more than buying it outright, and that math gets worse the scarcer the treatment.
