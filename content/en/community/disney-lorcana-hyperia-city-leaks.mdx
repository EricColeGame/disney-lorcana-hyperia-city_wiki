## Ink Drops: The Mechanic Driving Most of the Speculation

Ink Drops are the headline addition. They are consumable ink — tokens you spend to pay any ink cost demanded by a card or ability. They are generated through card abilities rather than placed in your inkwell the usual way, and the design team clearly intends them to be more than a simple ramp shortcut.

What makes the mechanic interesting is the number of hooks built around it. Some cards gain additional effects if you spend an Ink Drop to cast them, while others trigger based on whether Ink Drops are in play at all. Only two Ink Drop designs have been shown so far, which is a strong signal that the mechanic will expand across future sets.

| Interaction type | How it works | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Generation | Card abilities create Ink Drop cards | Turns otherwise quiet turns into ramp |
| Payment | Spend an Ink Drop for any ink cost a card or ability asks for | Flexible, color-agnostic acceleration |
| Cast triggers | Extra effects if you spend an Ink Drop to cast a card | Rewards sequencing over raw speed |
| Passive triggers | Abilities check whether Ink Drops are in play | Encourages holding resources instead of dumping them |
| Design space | Only two designs revealed | Expect more variety in later sets |

**Actionable tip:** treat Ink Drops as a resource you hold, not one you spend immediately. If a payoff card checks for Ink Drops in play, dumping them for a marginal discount can cost you a much bigger swing later in the same turn.

## Cinderella, Unintentional Icon: Full Card Breakdown

Cinderella joins Mickey Mouse, Best in Town as the second Iconic card of the set, and she is a genuine late-game engine rather than a flashy finisher.

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Ink cost | 7 |
| Inkable | Yes |
| Strength | 4 |
| Willpower | 6 |
| Shift cost | 5 ink |
| Ability | Bespoke Design |

Bespoke Design reads, in effect: at the end of your turn, you may look at the top two cards of your deck, put one on the top or bottom of your deck, and put the other into your inkwell face down and exerted. That last word does real work — the ink arrives exerted, so it cannot be used the turn it appears. What you get instead is card selection plus free ramp every single turn, which is the kind of grind that wins long games rather than short ones.

Community reports already frame her as a control-leaning Iconic. That is player experience rather than an official statement, but the logic holds: a 4/6 body that filters your deck and grows your inkwell is exactly what a slow deck wants.

## How to Vet Disney Lorcana Hyperia City Leaks Before You Share Them

Most bad information spreads because it is shared faster than it can be checked. Before you repeat a claim, run it through this filter:

- **Does it trace back to a Ravensburger channel?** The [official Disney Lorcana site](https://www.disneylorcana.com/) and official streams remain the only sources that confirm anything.
- **Is the image consistent?** Fake cards frequently reuse existing art, mismatch fonts, or place the collector number in the wrong corner.
- **Does the wording match Lorcana's rules language?** Community-made fakes tend to invent keyword phrasing that no existing card uses.
- **Who posted it first?** Reposts strip context. Find the original post before drawing conclusions.
- **Is the claim falsifiable?** "A new mechanic is coming" is unfalsifiable filler. "Cinderella costs seven ink" is checkable — and it checked out.

For anything that survives those five questions, still label it as unconfirmed in your own posts. The Disney Lorcana Hyperia City leaks cycle moves fast, and being the person who hedged correctly ages better than being the person who broke a fake card.
