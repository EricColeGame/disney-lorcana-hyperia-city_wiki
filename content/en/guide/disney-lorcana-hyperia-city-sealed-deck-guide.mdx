## Why Hyperia City Sealed Rewards Preparation

Sealed is the format where nobody buys a win. You open six booster packs, keep what you pull, and build a 40-card deck on the spot — no trading, no netdecking, no playset of the best card in the set. That is exactly why a **Disney Lorcana Hyperia City sealed deck guide** matters more than a constructed list: the skill being tested is card evaluation, not collection size. If you can rank 72 cards quickly and commit to two inks with a real plan, you will beat players with better pulls more often than you'd expect.

Community reports say the full Hyperia City card list has been revealed — all 242 cards, including the final batch of Enchanted and Epic rarities. Treat that as player experience rather than gospel until you're holding the cards, but the takeaway is real: you can study the pool before you sit down. A good **Disney Lorcana Hyperia City sealed deck guide** doesn't hand you a list. It teaches you the order of decisions that turns a random pile into a coherent deck.

## Before You Open a Single Pack

Most sealed losses happen before the first card hits the table. Walk in with a system.

- **Study the revealed list first.** Skim for the set's removal, its evasive threats, and any high-lore questers. You don't need memorization — you need pattern recognition so a strong card doesn't slip past you in a pile of commons.
- **Check the official rules if you're new to limited.** The [official Disney Lorcana site](https://www.disneylorcana.com) covers deck construction and format basics in plain language.
- **Bring sleeves, dice, and a lifepad.** Lore totals move fast and arguments cost more time than a notepad.
- **Practice the sort.** Six ink piles plus one "bomb" pile. Do it twice at home with any old packs and your build time drops by ten minutes.

The best **Disney Lorcana Hyperia City sealed deck guide** advice is boring: know what a good card looks like before you're under a clock. Sealed rewards the player who has already decided what "good" means.

## The 30-Minute Build Order

Build in passes, not in one long stare. Each pass has a single job.

| Step | Time | What you actually do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Sort by ink | 3 min | Six piles, face up, no evaluating yet |
| 2. Flag bombs and removal | 5 min | Pull anything that wins alone or kills cleanly |
| 3. Pick two inks | 6 min | Choose the pair with the deepest playable pool |
| 4. Fill the curve | 8 min | Lay out cost slots before you pick favorites |
| 5. Trim to 40–42 | 5 min | Cut the worst card, repeat |
| 6. Final check | 3 min | Count inkable cards, count removal, count lore |

A "bomb" in sealed is any card that demands an answer or generates two-for-one value on its own. Removal is any card that deals with a threat without needing a trade. Everything else is filler that has to justify its slot with stats, lore, or a keyword that matters.

### What good looks like at each cost

| Cost | What you want | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Cheap questers, early bodies, setup | Cards that do nothing on their own |
| 3 | Your best stat-to-cost ratio | Too many 3s clogging the top of your curve |
| 4–5 | Evasive threats, removal, value engines | Expensive cards with no immediate impact |
| 6+ | Game-enders only | Two or more clunky finishers you can't cast |

## Choosing Your Two Inks

You only get two inks, so this is the decision that shapes everything. Don't fall in love with your rarest card — fall in love with your deepest color. A rare with no support is a dead slot.

| Pair | What it does | Commit when |
|---|---|---|
| Amber + Steel | Goes wide, then grinds with removal | You have cheap questers and 3+ removal pieces |
| Ruby + Emerald | Fast, tricky, punishes slow starts | Your curve tops out at 5 and you have evasive bodies |
| Sapphire + Steel | Ramps, stabilizes, wins late | You opened big willpower and card draw |
| Amethyst + Ruby | Tempo with reach | You have bounce or draw plus aggressive questers |
| Amber + Emerald | Evasive lore race | Your deck can quest every turn without trading |
| Sapphire + Amethyst | Value and control | You have more card advantage than your curve needs |

There is no splashing in sealed. Two inks, full stop. A **Disney Lorcana Hyperia City sealed deck guide** can't pick your colors for you, but the rule is simple: count the playables. If your third-favorite ink has eight cards you'd happily start and your second-favorite has twelve, the choice is already made.

## Deck Composition, Curve, and In-Game Discipline

Most 40-card sealed decks look roughly like this. Deviate when your pool demands it, not because a card is shiny.

| Slot | Target count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Characters | 22–26 | Your engine — never go below 20 |
| Actions | 8–12 | Removal and tricks live here |
| Items | 0–3 | Only if they generate repeatable value |
| Locations | 0–3 | Great when they support your plan, dead weight otherwise |
| Inkable cards | 28+ | You need to hit your ink drops consistently |

### Curve targets

| Cost | Target | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3–5 | Don't overload — these are setup, not wins |
| 2 | 6–8 | Your most important turn |
| 3 | 7–9 | The backbone of sealed |
| 4 | 5–6 | Where value starts to compound |
| 5 | 3–4 | Your swing turn |
| 6+ | 2–3 | Finishers only |

### Three mistakes that cost games

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Over-inking | Dumping every card facedown for ink | Only ink when you have something to cast |
| Questing into bad trades | Racing when you're behind on board | Count lore per turn before you commit |
| Hoarding removal | Holding a kill spell all game | Use it on the card that's actually winning |

Play patterns matter as much as the build. Mulligan aggressively for a two- or three-cost play — a hand with no early action loses to a hand with two cheap questers almost every time. Quest when you're ahead on lore, challenge when you're behind on board, and remember that a character who quests every turn is worth more than one big trade. The rest of this **Disney Lorcana Hyperia City sealed deck guide** comes down to one habit: count the race, not the board.

## FAQ

**How many packs do I open in Hyperia City sealed?**
Six booster packs per player, which works out to roughly 72 cards before you cut down to a 40-card minimum deck. Check with your organizer — some casual events run different structures.

**Can I use three inks if my pool is strong in three colors?**
No. Sealed deck construction allows a maximum of two inks, so pick the two with the deepest playable pool rather than the two with the flashiest rares.

**Is this Disney Lorcana Hyperia City sealed deck guide useful for draft too?**
Yes. The evaluation framework — bombs, removal, curve, two-color discipline — transfers directly. Draft adds reading signals from other players, but the build order stays the same.

**What's the single biggest mistake new sealed players make?**
Playing too many colors' worth of "good cards" and too few inkable ones. A focused two-ink deck with a clean curve beats a pile of rares with no plan nearly every time.
