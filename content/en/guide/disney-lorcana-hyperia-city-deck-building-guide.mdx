## Start With a Plan, Not a Pile of Cards

The single biggest mistake in a new format is drafting a 60-card list before deciding how you intend to reach 20 lore. Hyperia City adds options, but options without a plan are just noise. Pick one of three plans first, then let the cards serve it.

| Plan | What it wants to do | Typical ink pairs | Speed | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lore rush | Quest every turn, skip most challenges | Amber/Amethyst, Amber/Ruby, Emerald/Amber | Fast | Sweepers and heavy removal |
| Midrange tempo | Trade efficiently early, quest ahead mid-game | Steel/Sapphire, Ruby/Steel, Emerald/Steel | Medium | Getting out-valued late |
| Control | Answer threats, win with a few big questers | Sapphire/Amethyst, Sapphire/Steel | Slow | Running out of clock vs. aggro |

Write your plan in one sentence before you open a deck builder. "I want to quest with two characters a turn and protect them with cheap tricks" is a plan. "I want to play all the good Hyperia City cards" is a wish.

## Choose an Ink Pairing You Can Actually Support

Lorcana decks use at most two ink types, need a minimum of 60 cards, and cap you at four copies of any card with the same name. That means your ink choice is the real identity of the deck — it decides which answers you simply do not have access to.

| Ink | Core identity | What it gives a Hyperia City list |
|---|---|---|
| Amber | Healing, support, going wide | Sticky boards and character-based lore engines |
| Amethyst | Card draw, bounce, trickery | Gas to keep questing and answers to big threats |
| Emerald | Evasion, debuffs, disruption | Unblockable questers and awkward attacks for opponents |
| Ruby | Aggression, damage, challenges | Reach and the ability to remove blockers |
| Sapphire | Ramp, items, card advantage | Faster ink and the strongest late-game engine |
| Steel | Damage, removal, sturdy bodies | Board control and characters that survive trades |

Pairings work best when one color supplies the engine and the other supplies the answer. Amber/Amethyst is the classic "go wide, draw cards" shell. Steel/Sapphire trades early and ramps into a backbreaking finish. Ruby/Steel is the removal-heavy midrange pairing that punishes sloppy sequencing.

Before you commit, check the [official Disney Lorcana site](https://www.disneylorcana.com/) for the current rules reference and set information — it is the cleanest way to confirm mechanics interactions before you build around them.

## Build the Curve Before You Fall in Love With Cards

A deck that cannot spend its ink every turn is a deck that loses to itself. Curve matters more than raw card quality in Lorcana because your inkwell grows by exactly one per turn, and every unspent ink is a tempo leak you never get back.

| Cost slot | Rough target (of 60) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1-cost | 6–10 | Early questers, cheap tricks, turn-one plays |
| 2-cost | 8–12 | The backbone: efficient bodies and setup |
| 3-cost | 8–12 | Mid-curve threats and key synergies |
| 4-cost | 6–9 | Stabilizers and value engines |
| 5-cost | 4–6 | Finishers and swing turns |
| 6+ cost | 2–4 | Game-ending payoffs only |

Two more numbers matter. First, keep uninkable cards low — player experience suggests staying in the 8–12 range, because a hand full of cards you cannot ink stalls your entire game. Second, count your turn-one and turn-two plays honestly. If you cannot act on turn one in half your opening hands, your curve is top-heavy.

## The Hyperia City Deck Building Workflow

Treat deck building as seven passes, not one long session. Each pass has a single job, which keeps you from endlessly fiddling with a list that has structural problems.

| Step | Action | Time | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write your one-sentence plan | 5 min | A win condition |
| 2 | Lock the ink pair | 5 min | Two colors, no waffling |
| 3 | Draft 12–16 core cards | 20 min | The non-negotiable shell |
| 4 | Fill the curve to 60 | 30 min | A playable first draft |
| 5 | Add interaction to taste | 15 min | Answers for common threats |
| 6 | Play 10 games, log results | 2 hrs | Real data |
| 7 | Cut the six weakest cards | 20 min | A tuned list |

Step 6 is where most players cheat. Ten games is not a huge sample, but it is enough to spot a curve hole or a card that sits in your hand every single time. Log your opening hands and whether you missed an ink drop — those two data points expose more problems than any theorycrafting session.

## Testing, Tuning, and the Traps That Cost You Games

A tuned deck is not a deck with better cards; it is a deck with fewer contradictions. Use the symptom table below to diagnose what your list is actually doing wrong.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Flooding on ink with nothing to play | Too few 2- and 3-cost cards | Shift 3–4 slots down the curve |
| Stuck with unplayable cards | Too many uninkables | Trim to 8–12 |
| Losing to one big threat | No hard answer in the pair | Add removal or bounce |
| Winning early, losing late | No card advantage | Add draw or a value engine |
| Great in testing, bad in events | Sideboard-style answers missing | Diversify interaction |

Two traps deserve a callout. The first is building around a single flashy card — if your plan collapses when you do not draw it, you do not have a plan. The second is copying a list without understanding its sequencing. Community reports and tournament results are useful references, but a deck you cannot pilot is worse than a homebrew you understand inside out.

This is the part of **Disney Lorcana Hyperia City deck building** that separates good players from great ones: they test with intent and change one variable at a time.

## Budget and Collection-Friendly Adjustments

Not everyone has a full playset of every rare, and that is fine. Most decks have three or four slots doing similar work, so substitutions rarely break a list if you preserve the role rather than the card.

- **Replace finishers with tempo.** If you lack a high-cost payoff, add two mid-cost questers that keep pressure on.
- **Trade rarity for consistency.** A common that fits your curve beats a rare that fights it.
- **Protect your core.** Never cut the 12–16 cards that make the deck function; cut the flex slots around them.
- **Rebuild slowly.** Change two cards, then play five games. Repeat.

A practical rule from player experience: if a swap costs you more than one turn of tempo, it is not a budget swap — it is a different deck.

## FAQ

**How many cards should a Hyperia City deck run?**
The minimum is 60, and most competitive lists stay at exactly 60 for consistency. Adding extra cards dilutes your best draws, so treat 60 as a target rather than a floor.

**Which ink pairing is best for a new player starting Disney Lorcana Hyperia City deck building?**
Amber/Amethyst and Ruby/Steel are forgiving starting points — one rewards going wide, the other rewards clean removal. Both teach sequencing without demanding perfect draws.

**How many games should I play before changing my list?**
Five to ten games is a reasonable first pass. Change one or two cards at a time so you can actually attribute the difference to your edit.

**Do I need every new Hyperia City card to compete?**
No. A focused list built around a clear plan routinely beats a pile of strong cards with no curve. Prioritize role coverage over raw card quality in this Disney Lorcana Hyperia City deck building format.
