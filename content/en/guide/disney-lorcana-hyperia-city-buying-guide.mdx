## The Hyperia City Release Calendar: Two Dates That Matter

Ravensburger is splitting the launch into two waves, and mixing them up is the single most common source of confusion in the weeks before a new set. Prerelease weekend is October 16, 2026, and it is limited to participating local game stores and select Disney Parks locations running their own in-store events. Nothing shows up in big-box retail that weekend.

General retail follows on October 23, 2026, when booster boxes, single packs, and the rest of the lineup reach shelves nationwide.

| Date | What Happens | Who Can Buy |
|---|---|---|
| October 16, 2026 | Prerelease weekend | Participating local game stores and select Disney Parks locations |
| October 23, 2026 | Full nationwide retail release | Everyone — big-box, online, and local shops |
| February 2027 | Into the Inkdark arrives with Collector Boosters | Not part of Hyperia City |

The Disney Parks channel is worth flagging because it gives collectors a second early-access route outside the traditional local-game-store network. If you live near a park running an event, that is a legitimate way to get product on the 16th rather than waiting a full week.

## Every Sealed Format in the Hyperia City Lineup

Five formats ship on day one, and each one targets a different kind of buyer rather than being a smaller or larger version of the same thing — a distinction most Disney Lorcana Hyperia City buying guide summaries gloss over. The main set runs 204 numbered cards, with the usual layer of rarer Enchanted and Iconic chase variants sitting on top.

| Format | What's Inside | Best For | Watch Out For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Booster Pack | 12 cards | Casual opening, impulse buys, draft nights | Lowest odds at chase cards per dollar spent |
| Booster Box | 24 packs (288 cards) | Volume chasers and set builders | Largest single upfront spend |
| PreRelease Box | Early-access product, roughly $39.99 MSRP, built around a sealed deck's worth of cards | Event players and anyone who wants cards before October 23 | Limited to stores running events; sign-up policies vary |
| Illumineer's Trove | 8 boosters plus reusable storage, dice, and a lore counter | Collectors who want accessories and a storage box | Not the cheapest route to raw cards |
| 2-Player Starter Set | Two complete 60-card decks | New players and kitchen-table play | Not a chase product; don't expect rares |

A quick math check helps here. A booster box gives you 288 cards in one purchase, while the Illumineer's Trove gives you 96 cards plus accessories. If your goal is cards, the box wins on raw volume every time. If your goal is a tidy shelf with dice and a lore counter included, the Trove earns its place.

First, if you are buying a starter set to actually play with, plan a display case or a sturdy deck box for it — starter product tends to get shelved loose and beaten up far more often than booster boxes do. Second, if you intend to keep a box sealed, buy the protection before launch week, not after.

## What Is Actually Inside Hyperia City

Beyond the Coco debut, the set is built around an urban metropolis that Ravensburger describes as where metro meets fantasy, with music, art, and hidden mysteries running through the theme. The Coco representation is the headline: Miguel Rivera, Hector Rivera, Dante, and Ernesto De La Cruz all receive their first-ever Lorcana cards here.

Hyperia City also lands shortly after a Lorcana format rotation following Attack of the Vine, which makes it one of the first sets designed around the newly rotated competitive pool. Even if you only care about Coco, that timing explains why Ravensburger slotted this release where it did rather than pushing it earlier in the calendar.

One expectation to kill now: Hyperia City does not include Collector Boosters. That format is confirmed to debut with Into the Inkdark in February 2027. If a listing promises Hyperia City Collector Boosters, it is wrong.

### Mistakes That Cost Collectors Money

- Confusing the October 16 prerelease with the October 23 general release and showing up to an empty big-box shelf.
- Paying a marked-up marketplace price without first checking Ravensburger's MSRP.
- Assuming the Illumineer's Trove is the cheapest way to maximize cards per dollar — a straight booster box usually wins that math.
- Waiting for Hyperia City Collector Boosters that were never announced for this set.
- Opening every box in launch week instead of holding one back while prices settle.
