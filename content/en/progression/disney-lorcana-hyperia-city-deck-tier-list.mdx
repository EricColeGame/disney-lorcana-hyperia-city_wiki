## How This Disney Lorcana Hyperia City Deck Tier List Is Built

Tier lists are only useful if you know what they're measuring. This one scores each archetype across six criteria, then weighs them against how the average player actually plays: at a local store, with a limited collection, and a couple of hours a week to test.

- **Consistency** — how often the deck does its thing by turn three or four
- **Interaction** — can it answer an opposing threat without falling behind?
- **Ink curve health** — how many dead cards sit in hand on turn two
- **Matchup spread** — does it beat one strategy or several?
- **Skill ceiling** — how much win rate comes from sequencing and reads
- **Cost to build** — how many high-demand cards it demands

One important caveat: Hyperia City is new, and the format is still settling. The rankings below reflect **community reports** and early **player experience** rather than a finished tournament season. Treat them as a starting hypothesis, not gospel, and re-check them after your first few locals.

## What Separates S-Tier From the Rest

### S-Tier: Ramp and Aggro Set the Pace

Ramp decks sit on top because Hyperia City rewards reaching a high ink count ahead of schedule. When you can play a premium threat while your opponent is still developing, card quality matters less than raw tempo. The archetype's weakness is obvious — it stumbles against decks that punish a slow first two turns — but its proactive plan is the most reliable thing in the format right now, according to **community reports**.

Aggro earns its S-rank for the opposite reason: it's cheap, fast, and punishes every greedy build. In early testing, the deck that ends games on turn five beats the deck that's still setting up.

### A-Tier: Powerful but Demanding

Control and tempo decks have higher ceilings than their tier suggests, but they ask more of the pilot. Control wants you to know exactly which threat to answer and which to ignore. Tempo wants you to sequence disruption perfectly, because one misstep flips a winning board into a losing one. If you're new to Lorcana, start elsewhere — but if you already understand the tempo of the game, these are the most rewarding decks in Hyperia City.

### B-Tier and C-Tier: Real Decks, Real Ceilings

B-tier builds are perfectly playable at a local level. Midrange value piles can beat anything on a good draw, and bounce engines generate absurd value when the pieces line up. The problem is variance: they have more "do nothing" hands than the top tiers. C-tier covers item engines and budget aggro — fun, instructive, and occasionally capable of stealing a match, but not where you want to be if your goal is a win-and-in.

### A Five-Step Testing Routine

Before you commit to a build, run it through this loop. It takes about two hours and will save you from buying cards you'll cut in a week.

| Step | Action | Time | What Success Looks Like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Goldfish 10 opening hands solo | 20 min | 7+ hands are playable by turn two |
| 2 | Play 5 games against the fastest deck you own | 30 min | You win at least 2 |
| 3 | Play 5 games against a control deck | 30 min | You don't run out of cards |
| 4 | Swap 4 cards and repeat step 2 | 20 min | Win rate improves, not just luck |
| 5 | Log every loss by cause | 20 min | One repeatable weakness emerges |

If you can't identify the single most common reason you lose, you haven't tested enough — you've just played. That distinction is the whole point of a tier list: it tells you where to look, not what to think.

## FAQ

**Is this Disney Lorcana Hyperia City deck tier list final?**

No. It's an early read based on community reports and testing at the local level. Expect the S-tier to hold roughly steady while the B and C tiers shuffle significantly once larger events produce results.

**Which deck should a beginner build first?**

Aggro. It's the cheapest tier list entry, it teaches combat math and quest timing, and it punishes you clearly enough that you learn fast. Move to ramp or control once you understand how games end.

**How many games before I trust my own results?**

Ten games is noise. Thirty games against at least three different archetypes gives you a signal you can act on. Track your losses by cause, not just your win rate.

**Do I need the most expensive cards to compete?**

No. Core engine cards matter far more than chase finishers, and a well-tuned budget build will beat a poorly sequenced expensive one most nights.

Start with one deck, test it honestly, and let the results — not the hype — tell you where it belongs on your own Disney Lorcana Hyperia City deck tier list.
