Hyperia City rewards players who move first and ask questions later, which is exactly the window an aggressive list is built to exploit. A well-tuned **Disney Lorcana Hyperia City aggro deck** turns cheap characters into a steady lore drip, forcing slower opponents to spend their turns answering threats instead of building their own board. ## Why Aggro Fits the Hyperia City Card Pool

Aggro in Disney Lorcana has never depended on a set's flashiest cards. It depends on redundancy: enough one-, two-, and three-cost characters that quest for meaningful lore, plus a handful of cards that protect them or push the final points through. Community reports around the Hyperia City reveal describe a large, 242-card pool, and player experience from previous set launches suggests that the low end of any new pool is usually deep enough to support a fast, consistent curve.

That matters because aggro is the cheapest archetype to build. You are not chasing a single mythic payoff — you are assembling twelve to sixteen interchangeable questers and letting math do the work.

Three pillars hold the strategy together:

- **Speed:** Deploy on turn one and never stop deploying. Every turn without a questing body is lore you cannot get back.
- **Redundancy:** If a card is essential, run enough copies that you see it reliably. If it is situational, cut it.
- **Reach:** Two or three effects that finish the game when the board stalls, whether that is direct damage, Rush, or an evasive threat the opponent cannot block.

Ruby and Emerald shells reward players who already understand combat math and want to close games with evasion rather than raw width.

## Curve Targets for a Hyperia City Aggro Deck

Most aggro lists fail not because the cards are wrong, but because the curve is. A 60-card deck that plays four one-drops and twelve five-drops is a midrange deck wearing an aggro costume. Use the targets below as a starting skeleton, then adjust after ten to fifteen test games.

| Ink cost | Suggested count | Job in the deck |
|---|---|---|
| 1-cost | 8–10 | Quest early, chip in lore, bait out removal |
| 2-cost | 10–12 | The engine of the deck; evasive or high-lore bodies |
| 3-cost | 8–10 | Pressure plus a relevant ability or protection |
| 4-cost | 4–6 | Stabilizers or big questers that end the game |
| 5+ cost | 2–4 | Finishers only; everything here must close |
| Actions and songs | 8–10 | Removal, draw, and tempo plays |

Two numbers matter more than any single card choice. First, keep the deck's uninkable count in the low teens so you rarely brick on ink. Second, count how much lore your average opening hand can produce by turn three — if the answer is fewer than five or six points, the list is too slow.

## Role Slots to Fill in the Deck

Rather than copying a list card for card, build by role. Every successful aggro build fills the same seven jobs, and the Hyperia City pool will offer multiple candidates for each.

| Role | What to look for | How many |
|---|---|---|
| Turn-one quester | Cheap character with lore value and no setup cost | 4–6 |
| Evasive two-drop | Character that quests past grounded blockers | 4–6 |
| Lore engine | Character that gains extra lore or rewards questing | 3–4 |
| Protection | Ward, Bodyguard, or resist to keep questers alive | 3–4 |
| Reach | Rush, direct damage, or unblockable pressure | 2–3 |
| Refill | Card draw or hand filtering so you never stall | 3–5 |
| Removal | Cheap answers to the opponent's key threat | 4–6 |

Locations are worth a specific note. Hyperia City is a city, and if the set leans into location cards the way recent Lorcana releases have, locations can function as passive lore sources in an aggro shell — but only if they cost little and do not slow your character deployment. Treat them as a supplement, not a replacement for bodies.

## Turn-by-Turn Game Plan

The single biggest difference between a good pilot and a great one is knowing which turn you are allowed to stop questing and start trading. The table below outlines the default plan for a **Disney Lorcana Hyperia City aggro deck**.

| Turn | Priority | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Play a one-cost quester | Quest immediately if it survives; force an answer |
| 2 | Two characters or one two-drop | Favor board width over a single bigger body |
| 3 | Quest with everything, hold one blocker | Start tracking your opponent's removal mana |
| 4 | Add pressure, challenge only to clear a blocker | Do not trade questers for no reason |
| 5 | Push for 12–15 lore | Use reach effects if the board stalls |
| 6+ | Close the game | Every card must either quest or finish |

For mulligans, keep hands with two or three cheap characters and at least one card you are happy to ink. Ship back hands that require four ink before they do anything — aggro loses more games to slow keeps than to bad draws.

## Matchups and Adjustments

Aggro does not play the same way against every opponent. Use the quick reference below to decide whether you are the beatdown or whether you need to slow down and protect your board.

| Opponent style | Your plan | Cards to bring in |
|---|---|---|

## Five Mistakes That Sink Aggro Runs

1. **Over-questing into open removal.** If your opponent held three ink and passed, assume a removal spell is waiting.
2. **Playing too many expensive finishers.** Every five-drop you add is a turn you are not questing.
3. **Ignoring the inkable count.** Uninkable cards feel powerful until three of them sit dead in your hand.
4. **Challenging without a reason.** Trading a questing body for a defensive body usually helps the opponent.
5. **Skipping the refill package.** An aggro deck with no card draw loses to its own empty hand around turn six.

## FAQ

**How many lore-producing characters should a Disney Lorcana Hyperia City aggro deck run?**
Aim for roughly 28 to 34 characters that can quest, with most of them costing three ink or less. The rest of the deck should be removal, protection, and draw so those characters keep converting into lore.

**Is aggro the best archetype for climbing with Hyperia City?**
It is the fastest archetype to learn and the cheapest to build, which makes it excellent for early progression. Whether it stays dominant depends on how the wider metagame settles once players have tested the full pool.

**How long should a game last with this deck?**
Ideally six to eight turns. If your matches routinely run past turn nine, your curve is too high or you are missing reach effects.

**Do I need expensive cards to make it work?**
No. Aggro rewards consistency over power. A focused list of cheap, redundant questers will outperform a pile of high-rarity cards that do not curve out.

Start with the curve, fill the role slots, and test ten games before changing anything. A disciplined **Disney Lorcana Hyperia City aggro deck** wins because it does the simple thing every single turn — and that is a habit worth building.
