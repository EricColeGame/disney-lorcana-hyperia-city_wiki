Disney Lorcana's next set trades the wilderness for a neon-lit metropolis, and the first wave of spoilers shows exactly how the game plans to get there. If you want to stay a step ahead of your local meta, understanding the Disney Lorcana Hyperia City card abilities revealed so far is the fastest shortcut available. The set leans hard into Pixar's *Coco*, and those new Disney Lorcana Hyperia City card abilities reward players who build around songs, discard piles, and cheap Evasive characters instead of raw stats.

That shift matters because Lorcana's previous expansions pushed players toward board-heavy, character-driven play. Hyperia City looks like it wants the opposite: cheap characters that generate lore from the *act* of singing, plus discard-pile payoffs that turn your graveyard into a second hand. Here's everything we know, how the pieces fit, and how to start testing before release day.

### Miguel Rivera, Twice Over

"Promising Musician" is the simplest engine in the batch: an Amber Storyborn Hero with Crowd Pleaser, which converts every song it sings into a lore. Note the wording — the trigger is *singing*, not playing a song. That means you want Miguel on the board and exerted for songs rather than sitting back as a vanilla body.

"Astonishing Musician" is the upgrade path. It's a Dreamborn Hero you can play directly on top of another Miguel card, which is a stack-on-top pattern that echoes Shift without the reveal confirming that keyword. Its Transcendent Performance ability returns a character from your discard pile to hand when it enters play, so the two Miguels form a natural curve: sing early, upgrade, then recycle a threat or a utility character you lost.

### "Though I Have To Say Goodbye" and the Song Payoff

This Ruby Action/Song is named after a lyric from "Remember Me," and the artwork shows Ernesto De La Cruz mid-performance. Mechanically it's the most interesting card in the batch because it does two things at once.

First, it's cheap to sing: a character with a cost of 2 or more can sing it for free, which puts it in range of almost any early board. Second, its effect digs through the top 3 cards of your deck and converts each song discarded that way into +1 Strength on a character you choose. In a song-dense build, that's a combat trick that also filters your deck — and it fills your discard pile for the Dante and Miguel payoffs at the same time.

### Dante's Two Very Different Jobs

"Enthusiastic Stray" is an aggressive tempo Ally: when it's played, a chosen character gets Challenger +2 for the turn, which the reveal also frames as +2 Strength while challenging. In practice, that turns a modest attacker into a trade winner or lets a small character punch above its weight for one combat.

"Strange and Endearing" is the opposite — a grind card. It has Evasive, meaning only other Evasive characters can challenge it, and Digging In The Trash adds 1 lore while you have 10 or more cards in your discard pile. Ten cards is a real threshold, but it's easy to hit by turn five in a deck that discards songs deliberately.

### Ernesto De La Cruz and Héctor Rivera

Ernesto De La Cruz – Idol of Millions is a Storyborn Villain who can sing songs at a cost of 5, and his Top of the Charts ability gives you +1 lore if your opponent has a song card in their discard pile. That's a genuinely unusual condition — it punishes opponents who cycle songs early and does nothing against decks that never discard them, so treat him as a metagame call rather than a staple.

Héctor Rivera – Worldwide Sensation was announced but its ability text hasn't been shared yet. Given the family-and-music theme, expect a song or discard interaction, but don't build around him until the full spoiler lands.

## How the Disney Lorcana Hyperia City Card Abilities Work Together

Read as a group, these Disney Lorcana Hyperia City card abilities describe a deck with two engines that feed each other. The first is the song engine: cheap characters sing songs, Miguel converts those songs into lore, and "Though I Have To Say Goodbye" filters your deck while it pumps a character. The second is the discard engine: every song that goes to your discard pile powers Dante's lore trigger and Miguel's recursion.

| Synergy | Cards involved | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Song-to-lore conversion | Miguel – Promising Musician + cheap songs | Every sing is a lore, so songs effectively read as "gain 1 extra lore" |
| Recursion loop | Miguel – Astonishing Musician + discard payoff | Returns a lost character while your discard count climbs |
| Deck filtering | Though I Have To Say Goodbye + song-heavy list | Discards songs from the top 3 for Strength and fuels graveyard thresholds |
| Sticky lore | Dante – Strange and Endearing + discard build | Evasive body that adds lore once you cross 10 cards in discard |
| Tempo swing | Dante – Enthusiastic Stray + small challengers | Turns a trade into a favorable one for a single turn |

The tension is real, though. Songs in Lorcana are typically played by exerting a character, so a song deck risks leaving itself open on your opponent's turn. Community reports from earlier song-focused archetypes suggest the fix is to run more cheap bodies than you think you need — you want to exert a *spare* character for the song, not your best questing threat.

## Deck-Building Tips for the New Abilities

Start by deciding which engine you're actually building. Splitting evenly between song payoff and discard payoff usually produces a deck that does neither well.

- **Prioritize singer bodies over payoffs early.** A cost-2 character that can sing "Though I Have To Say Goodbye" for free is worth more than a second copy of a lore engine you can't cast.
- **Count your songs before adding discard payoffs.** Dante's Digging In The Trash wants 10+ cards in the discard pile; songs are the cheapest way to get there without giving up card advantage.
- **Treat Ernesto as a sideboard card.** Top of the Charts is dead against opponents who never discard songs, so main-deck him only if your local scene is song-heavy.
- **Don't over-value recursion.** Miguel's Transcendent Performance returns a character to hand, not to play — you still pay for it again.
- **Test at prerelease.** Sealed pools will show you which of these Disney Lorcana Hyperia City card abilities actually close games, and which just look good on paper.

| Discard grind | Dante – Strange and Endearing, filtering songs | Incremental lore + Evasive chip | Slow starts |
| Aggro splash | Dante – Enthusiastic Stray, cheap challengers | Favorable trades into questing | Runs out of gas |
| Recursion value | Miguel – Astonishing Musician | Rebuying high-impact characters | Card disadvantage if overused |

## FAQ

**When does Disney Lorcana: Hyperia City release?**
Prerelease begins October 16, 2026 at local hobby stores and Disney Parks, with the global release following on October 23, 2026.

**Which of the Disney Lorcana Hyperia City card abilities matter most competitively?**
Miguel Rivera – Promising Musician and "Though I Have To Say Goodbye" look like the strongest pair, because they convert cheap songs into lore and Strength at the same time. Dante – Strange and Endearing is the best standalone grinder thanks to Evasive plus a lore trigger.

**Is Hyperia City only about *Coco*?**
No. The reveals so far highlight *Coco* characters, but the set is 204 cards and the story is set in a full metropolis, so expect other Disney and Pixar franchises to appear.

**How does singing work with Miguel Rivera?**
Singing a song requires exerting a character whose cost meets or exceeds the song's cost. Miguel's Crowd Pleaser triggers on that action, giving you 1 lore each time — so every song in a Miguel deck effectively carries a small lore rider.
