## How Lorcana Rarity Tiers Work

Every Disney Lorcana Hyperia City rarity guide worth reading starts with the symbol printed near the bottom of the card, just above the collector number. That symbol is the fastest way to sort a stack of pulls.

| Rarity Tier | How Common It Is | What It Means for Collectors |
|---|---|---|
| Common | Most frequent pull | Bulk. Fine for Sealed and Draft, minimal resale value |
| Uncommon | Less frequent than Common | Deck filler; small value outside of competitive standouts |
| Rare | Scarcer than Uncommon | Where many constructed staples and mid-tier chases live |
| Super Rare | Scarcer than Rare | Higher price ceiling; often format-defining |
| Legendary | Scarce, headline tier | The chase cards of the set; biggest non-foil prices |
| Enchanted | Rarest tier, alternate art | Collector-only chase, traditionally printed outside the main numbering |

Rarity does not equal power. Some of the most played cards in Lorcana history have been Uncommons, and some Legendaries have never found a home in a competitive deck. What rarity controls is supply — how many copies exist relative to demand.

### The Enchanted Card Numbering Trick

Here's a detail that trips up newer collectors: Enchanted cards in Lorcana are traditionally numbered *above* the set total, appearing as 205/204 and upward. That means the "204-card set" figure describes the main numbered checklist, not the total number of collectible cards in Hyperia City.

No Hyperia City Enchanted card has been revealed at the time of writing. When they start appearing, expect them to sit outside that 1–204 range and to command the highest prices in the set regardless of what the base version does.

## The Three Confirmed Cards, Ranked by Collector Interest

For anyone using this Disney Lorcana Hyperia City rarity guide to plan a binder, here's how the confirmed cards stack up.

- **Miguel Rivera – Street Musician (Amber Rare).** The highest-priority pull of the three. He's a Rare, he's the emotional center of *Coco*, and he's one of the very first *Coco* glimmers ever printed. First appearances don't guarantee lasting value, but they do guarantee a permanent place in the game's history.
- **Un Poco Loco (Amethyst Rare).** The most interesting card structurally. It confirms Ravensburger isn't limiting *Coco* to character glimmers — songs are in the mix too. That opens the door to a much deeper *Coco* collection than a two-character cameo would suggest.
- **Héctor Rivera – Street Musician (Ruby Uncommon).** The lowest rarity of the three, but the most mechanically relevant so far. **Singer 3** lets him exert to sing a song as though he had cost 3, and **Strike a Chord** puts the top card of your deck into your discard pile when he's played. That's a real deck-building hook, not just binder fodder.

A useful rule of thumb: Rares and above drive collector pricing, while Uncommons drive competitive demand. Héctor is the card most likely to show up in actual decks; Miguel is the card most likely to hold long-term fan value.

## Rarity Is Only Half the Story: What Actually Drives Value

That's why a Disney Lorcana Hyperia City rarity guide should never stop at the rarity symbol. Five factors do most of the work.

| Factor | Why It Moves Price | Hyperia City Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Rarity tier | Caps the available supply | Two Rares confirmed; no Legendary or Enchanted yet |
| Competitive playability | Creates demand from players, not just collectors | Héctor's Singer 3 and Strike a Chord |
| Character popularity | Emotional demand from franchise fans | Miguel and Héctor are *Coco*'s leads |
| First appearance | Permanent historical demand | *Coco*'s Lorcana debut |
| Foil and treatment | Multiplies the base card's value | Foil versions of any confirmed Rare |

Community reports from earlier Lorcana launches consistently show the same pattern: a card's price peaks in the first two weeks after release, then settles as tournament results and pack supply catch up. If you're buying singles rather than opening product, patience usually pays.

There's also a franchise effect specific to this set. Hyperia City is where *Coco* begins in Disney Lorcana, and Ravensburger has described the set as a modern city meeting fantasy, with music and art front and center. That thematic overlap makes a deeper *Coco* presence likely — but seeing a character in promotional artwork does not confirm a card in the numbered set. Only official reveals do.
